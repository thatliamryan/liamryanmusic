ABOUT LIAM

Music is fun!

I was born and raised in Chicago, Illinois, where my musical journey began. I went on to earn a Bachelor of Music in Jazz Vocal Performance from the Frost School of Music at the University of Miami. More recently, I received my Master of Music in Jazz Vocal Performance from Manhattan School of Music. During my graduate studies, I also studied pedagogy under Jim Saltzman, whose work in music education helped shape my perspective on teaching and learning. Alongside my own education as a musician, teaching has become an important part of my work. I have spent the past four years tutoring students in both computer science and music theory. Working across these two subjects has given me experience helping students approach complicated material in different ways and finding explanations that make difficult concepts feel more manageable.

I work with students of all ages and experience levels. Whether you're a complete beginner learning the fundamentals of music theory an advanced student working through coursework, or an adult returning to music after time away, lessons are made to fit your individual goals. I also work with students preparing for AP music theory, general exams, auditions, placement tests, and other academic requirements. I provide long-term instruction to help my students prepare for a specific assignment or an upcoming exam.

Music theory is a cumulative subject. Many concepts build directly on one another, which means that a gap in understanding can make later material unnecessarily difficult. In a traditional classroom, however, the course continues at a fixed pace. If a student doesn't fully understand one topic before the class moves forward, those gaps can gradually build. My approach is centered around identifying those gaps and giving students the time they need to genuinely understand the material. Student's will often bring homework, study guides, or past exams in order to review material that was unclear to them. Lessons move according to the student's needs.

My goal isn't simply to help students get through the next exam or assignment. I want them to develop an understanding of music theory that they can continue to use independently.
